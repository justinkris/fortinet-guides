Session 02 Chapter 01 — Network Security Architecture

Design Secure Networks Using Fortinet

How to segment, zone, and architect a resilient enterprise network using Fortinet solutions.

Before You Begin · Real-World Scenario

The Flat Network That Became a Highway for Attackers

Fieldstone Retail operated 80 convenience stores across three states. Their IT infrastructure had grown organically over a decade — each store ran a single network switch connecting POS terminals, staff laptops, the back-office PC, a surveillance DVR, a connected printer, and a wireless access point for staff phones. Everything shared the same /24 subnet. At the corporate level, a /16 aggregated all 80 stores and the headquarters LAN into one flat address space. Every device could reach every other device. The cost was low, the configuration simple, and the whole arrangement had worked fine — right up until a Tuesday in October when a third-party refrigeration maintenance vendor connected a laptop to the Ethernet port in a break room at Store #34 to run a remote diagnostic.

The vendor's laptop was running unpatched Windows 10 and was already compromised with credential-harvesting malware. Within four hours of connecting, the malware had swept the local /24, found the POS terminals, and used a known vulnerability in the POS application to extract track data from RAM. Because the network was flat, the malware's lateral movement was unimpeded — it crossed from Store #34's subnet to Store #12 via a misconfigured routing rule at headquarters without triggering a single firewall alert. By morning, POS data from 61 of the 80 stores had been exfiltrated. The forensic firm brought in two weeks later identified that the initial compromise of the vendor laptop was unrelated to Fieldstone's network — but once inside their flat infrastructure, the attacker had unlimited lateral movement.

The lead network architect reviewed the topology diagram during the incident debrief and felt a specific, sinking clarity. There was no segmentation design — not because someone had made a bad decision, but because no deliberate network design had been made at all. The stores had been provisioned by copying a template from the first deployment, and the template had no zones, no inter-segment enforcement, and no path control. A proper Fortinet design would have placed POS terminals on an isolated VLAN with only outbound payment processor traffic allowed, vendor access on a quarantine segment requiring explicit authentication, and the DVR on a separate IoT zone with no routing to the corporate LAN. Inter-VLAN policies on a FortiGate would have blocked every lateral movement step the malware took. This session is about learning how to make that design — the zones, the segments, and the enforcement points.

Overview

Network segmentation is the practice of dividing a network into isolated zones where traffic between zones is explicitly controlled and inspected. The goal is to limit blast radius — even when one zone is compromised, the attacker cannot freely traverse to other zones. FortiGate implements segmentation through VLAN subinterfaces and security zones, allowing a single physical appliance to enforce policies between dozens of logical network segments without requiring multiple physical firewalls.

A Fortinet reference architecture for a campus or enterprise site typically includes at least four zones: a User zone for employee endpoints, a Server/DMZ zone for internally accessible servers, a Guest zone for untrusted user devices, and an OT/IoT zone for operational technology and connected devices. The Internet-facing perimeter sits between the WAN interface and all internal zones. FortiGate applies firewall policies with security profiles at every inter-zone boundary — so even traffic that is "permitted" is still inspected for threats.

At the branch and multi-site level, Fortinet's SD-WAN capability allows FortiGate to steer traffic intelligently across multiple WAN paths — broadband, MPLS, LTE — based on application performance metrics rather than static routing. This replaces expensive MPLS-only designs with cost-effective hybrid WAN while maintaining policy enforcement. The FortiSwitch and FortiAP ecosystem integrates directly into the FortiGate Security Fabric, giving the firewall visibility into wired switch ports and wireless clients as first-class managed entities rather than black-box external devices.
