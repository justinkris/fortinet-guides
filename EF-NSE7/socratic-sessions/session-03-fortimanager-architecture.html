Session 03 Chapter 02 — Central Management

FortiManager Architecture

How FortiManager centralizes policy, device configuration, and change management at enterprise scale.

Before You Begin · Real-World Scenario

The Policy That Went to the Wrong Customer

Nexar Managed Security was growing fast. In eighteen months they had signed 30 enterprise customers — hospitals, law firms, regional banks — and they managed every customer's FortiGate estate from a single FortiManager instance. The platform was powerful and the team was skilled, but in the pressure of rapid growth one architectural decision had been skipped: ADOMs had never been configured. All 30 customer device sets lived in the default ADOM. Policy packages were named by customer abbreviation — HOSP-01, LAWFIRM-02, BANK-03 — and the convention had always been enough. Until a Tuesday afternoon in March when a junior engineer, working under deadline pressure to deploy an emergency policy update for a hospital client, selected the wrong policy package from the dropdown.

The engineer installed the hospital's policy package to three FortiGates belonging to Meridian Capital, a regional investment bank, at 2:47 PM. Meridian's firewall policies were replaced wholesale. Their outbound trading platform traffic, which had been permitted via carefully crafted application control rules built over two years, was now subject to the hospital's far more restrictive HIPAA-aligned policy set. Within four minutes, Meridian's traders began losing connectivity to their order management system. By 3:00 PM the Meridian security team was on the phone with Nexar's helpdesk. At 3:08 PM, Nexar's senior engineer was staring at a FortiManager audit log trying to understand which policy had been installed where and when. The rollback took eleven minutes — eleven minutes during which equity traders at a regional bank could not execute orders.

The post-incident review was technically straightforward but organizationally painful. In a properly configured FortiManager deployment for an MSSP, each customer exists in its own ADOM. Policies, devices, address objects, and administrator access are all scoped to the ADOM — it is structurally impossible to install Customer A's policy to Customer B's devices from within the customer A ADOM. The junior engineer would have seen only the hospital's devices in the policy install wizard. The architecture that prevents this kind of error is not a workaround or a best practice — it is the designed behavior of the ADOM system. This session is about understanding that architecture from first principles.

Overview

FortiManager is Fortinet's centralized management platform for FortiGate and other Fortinet devices. At its core, it solves a scale problem: when a single organization runs dozens or hundreds of FortiGates, managing each device individually through its own GUI becomes unmanageable, error-prone, and audit-unfriendly. FortiManager centralizes policy authoring, configuration storage, and software deployment into a single system with a full audit trail and change workflow.

The fundamental organizational unit in FortiManager is the ADOM — Administrative Domain. An ADOM is a management container that scopes everything within it: which devices belong to it, which policy packages apply to those devices, which administrators can see and modify it, and which address/service objects exist in its namespace. For MSSPs, each customer gets its own ADOM. For large enterprises, ADOMs can segregate by region, business unit, or device type. The key insight is that ADOMs provide hard administrative boundaries — not just naming conventions, but enforced structural isolation.

Within an ADOM, the central workflow concept is the policy package. A policy package is a named set of firewall policies stored in FortiManager's database — it is the authoritative source of policy for the devices it is assigned to. Changes are made to the policy package in FortiManager, then explicitly installed to target devices. This separation of "authored in FortiManager" from "running on device" introduces a change workflow with review, approval, and rollback capability. Understanding the relationship between policy packages, device databases, and the install process is foundational to every FortiManager topic on the NSE7 exam.
