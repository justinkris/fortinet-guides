Session 25 Chapter 08 — Auto-Discovery VPN

ADVPN Shortcut Tunnels

The shortcut request/response flow — how spoke-to-spoke tunnels form, persist, and tear down dynamically.

Before You Begin · Real-World Scenario

ADVPN Configured, Shortcuts Never Formed

Northgate Financial Services had a clean ADVPN deployment project: 20 spoke offices connecting to a pair of hub FortiGates in an active/passive configuration. The project engineer had done this before — ADVPN documentation was familiar territory. Hub phase1 interfaces were configured with the correct auto-discovery flags. BGP was peering cleanly between hub and all 20 spokes. The ADVPN overlay tunnels were all established. The project passed acceptance testing. The deployment was declared successful. Two weeks later, a capacity review showed that spoke-to-spoke traffic was still routing through the hub. Not a little of it — all of it. The shortcut tunnel table on every spoke was empty. Shortcuts had never formed, not once, on any spoke, against any destination.

The network team spent several days investigating. They verified the hub configuration obsessively: set auto-discovery-sender enable was present on both hub phase1 interfaces. Hub BGP was advertising all spoke prefixes correctly — the routing tables on every spoke showed the right prefixes via hub next-hops. IKE debugs on the hub showed the hub was generating shortcut offers and sending them toward the destination spokes when spoke-to-spoke traffic crossed the hub. The IKE packets were leaving the hub. The BGP sessions were healthy. The spoke tunnels were up. On paper, everything required for shortcuts was present. The team opened a TAC case. After exchanging debugs over two days, the TAC engineer asked a quiet question: could the team share the full phase1 configuration from a representative spoke, not just the flags they had verified?

The TAC engineer read the spoke phase1 output and found the problem in under sixty seconds. set auto-discovery-receiver enable was absent from every spoke. The hub was sending shortcut offers — IKE messages inviting the destination spoke to establish a direct tunnel with the initiating spoke. The destination spokes were receiving those IKE messages and silently discarding them because the receiver flag was not configured. Without that flag, a spoke's IKE daemon treats shortcut offer messages as unexpected and drops them without logging a meaningful error. The shortcut was being offered and rejected thousands of times per day with no trace on the spoke side. Adding set auto-discovery-receiver enable to all 20 spokes took under an hour with FortiManager. Within minutes of the push, the shortcut tunnel tables began populating. By end of day, the hub was forwarding less than 8% of spoke-to-spoke traffic — the remainder was flowing via direct shortcuts.

Overview

An ADVPN shortcut is a dynamically negotiated IPsec tunnel between two spoke FortiGates that forms on demand when traffic flows between them. Unlike the permanent hub tunnels that are configured statically and maintained continuously, shortcut tunnels do not exist until traffic triggers their creation, and they are torn down after a configurable idle period passes with no traffic. The shortcut establishment process is an IKE exchange facilitated by the hub: the hub detects spoke-to-spoke traffic, sends a shortcut offer to the destination spoke, the destination spoke accepts and returns its connection information, and the two spokes complete the IKE negotiation directly between themselves — the hub steps out of the data plane from that point forward.

The exact sequencing of the shortcut establishment process matters for both operations and exam purposes. When Spoke A sends traffic to Spoke B's subnet, the first packets are forwarded through the hub (since that is the only path available). The hub's ADVPN logic detects this transit traffic and sends an IKE shortcut offer to Spoke B, including Spoke A's tunnel endpoint address. Spoke B, if configured with set auto-discovery-receiver enable, accepts the offer and begins IKE negotiation directly with Spoke A. Spoke A, if configured with set auto-discovery-forwarder enable, completes the IKE exchange. Once the shortcut is established, subsequent packets from Spoke A to Spoke B use the direct tunnel. The hub continues forwarding the initial packets during this negotiation window — typically a few hundred milliseconds — before the shortcut takes over.

Shortcut tunnels have a finite lifetime governed by idle timeouts. When no traffic flows through a shortcut for the configured idle period (default 15 minutes), the shortcut is torn down and the IPsec security associations are cleared. The next packet from Spoke A to Spoke B will again traverse the hub and trigger a new shortcut negotiation. This behavior keeps the shortcut table lean in large deployments where most spoke pairs rarely communicate — only the active spoke-to-spoke pairs maintain shortcuts at any given time. Understanding the full lifecycle — formation, forwarding, idle detection, teardown, re-negotiation — is essential for ADVPN troubleshooting and is a recurring focus of NSE7 examination questions.
